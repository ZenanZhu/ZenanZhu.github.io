Published research · 2022

State estimation under sensor misalignment

Invariant extended Kalman filtering

I developed an augmented invariant extended Kalman filter (InEKF) that explicitly estimates IMU-to-segment placement offsets while estimating body motion. The approach combines inertial sensing with leg kinematics to reduce the effects of imperfect sensor placement. This project now groups both the extended journal version and the earlier ACC conference paper.

State estimationLie groupsSensor fusion
Technical details +

My contribution

Developed the augmented state representation, sensor-misalignment model, and filtering formulation; investigated observability and evaluated the estimator against an existing contact-aided InEKF.

Method & validation

The deterministic process model preserves a group-affine structure. Inertial data and stance-leg kinematic constraints provide complementary information. The journal study evaluated three participants during squatting, rolling-foot walking, and ladder climbing.

Result & scope

The proposed filter improved velocity estimation and convergence in the reported experiments. Its measurement model is not in an invariant observation form; absolute position and yaw are not observable from the available measurements. These results are not a claim of globally guaranteed full-state convergence.

Published research · RA-L 2026

Fast adaptation for gait & terrain estimation

Model-agnostic meta-learning

I co-developed a multi-output learning framework that estimates gait phase, locomotion mode, and terrain incline from four fabric-based sensors. Meta-learning trains an initialization that adapts to unseen users with a small labeled calibration set.

Meta-learningTime-series learningWearable sensing
Technical details +

My contribution

Co-first-author work on a MAML-based estimation framework, including model development and experimental evaluation using wearable soft-sensor data.

Method & validation

A shared one-dimensional convolutional network feeds three prediction heads. The locomotion-mode estimate also informs gait-phase and incline predictions. Evaluation uses leave-one-subject-out testing across nine participants and five locomotion modes.

Reported results

The final paper reports mean gait-phase accuracy of 85%, locomotion-mode accuracy of 100%, and incline RMSE of 2.67° with 3.5 seconds of labeled calibration data per locomotion/speed condition and four fine-tuning steps (Table I). These are results within the reported evaluation, not guarantees for new deployments.

Adaptation remains supervised. The study evaluates estimation; it does not establish a reduction in effort or metabolic cost from exoskeleton assistance.

RL for human–exoskeleton simulation

Policy training & replay with MyoAssist

I am training and replaying locomotion policies for a simulated human model coupled to Dephy ExoBoots using MyoAssist. This ongoing work extends my research toward learning-based control of physically coupled human–robot systems.

Reinforcement learningMusculoskeletal simulationMyoAssist
Technical details +

Current work

Running reinforcement-learning experiments and inspecting policy rollouts for the coupled human–exoskeleton simulation.

Framework credit

This work uses the open-source MyoAssist framework from the NeuMove Lab. The framework and its underlying models are credited to their original developers; they are not presented as my own software framework.

Scope

The replay is a simulation demonstration. It does not by itself establish reduced muscle effort, better assistance, robustness across conditions, or successful transfer to physical hardware.

Poster presentation · IROS 2023

Auto-calibrating joint-angle estimation

Extended Kalman filtering with fabric sensors

I developed an EKF-based approach that maps raw fabric-sensor measurements to joint angles while estimating sensor calibration parameters. The estimator combines soft-sensor signals, exoskeleton IMU data, and lower-limb kinematics.

EKFOnline calibrationJoint kinematics
Technical details +

My contribution

Formulated an estimator that augments joint angles with sensor-to-angle gains and offsets, and uses IMU dynamics and foot-contact kinematics to constrain estimation.

Preliminary validation

The IROS 2023 poster presents squatting experiments with motion-capture reference data. One scenario uses motion-capture-derived angles as estimator inputs; the raw-fabric-sensor scenario estimates the left knee angle.

Scope

The formulation targets hip and knee estimation, but the reported raw-sensor validation does not demonstrate simultaneous estimation of all four joints. The linear sensor-to-angle assumption is an approximation, not a complete model of fabric-sensor hysteresis.

AIM 2024 poster · Ongoing extension

Soft-sensor-driven exoskeleton control

Gait-event timing & personalized assistance

I implemented a preliminary gait-event-based ankle torque interface using hip and knee fabric sensors, and proposed a personalized control framework combining Transformer-based gait recognition with meta-learning.

Exoskeleton controlGait eventsPersonalization
Technical details +

My contribution

Developed a soft-sensor-based interface for gait-event detection and polynomial ankle torque profiles. The broader proposed framework combines gait recognition and meta-learning to adapt assistance to biomechanics and user preference.

Preliminary validation

The AIM 2024 work reports a preliminary treadmill demonstration with Dephy ExoBoots and motion-capture reference timing, illustrating the connection between fabric-sensor measurements and commanded torque.

Scope

The preliminary hardware demonstration and the proposed personalization framework are distinct stages. Biomechanical benefits and the effectiveness of the complete personalization framework require further validation.

Modeling study · Preliminary

Reduced-order human–exoskeleton dynamics

Mechanics-based modeling & parameter identification

I derived a reduced-order model of the coupled human–exoskeleton system to estimate center-of-mass motion and support model-based planning of assistive torque. This work connects interpretable dynamics with wearable-robot control design.

DynamicsReduced-order modelingSystem identification
Technical details +

My contribution

Derived reduced-order dynamics for the coupled system and investigated spring-loaded inverted-pendulum (SLIP) approximations, contact assumptions, and recursive least-squares parameter identification.

Modeling questions

The work examines how model parameters and foot-contact assumptions affect center-of-mass predictions, and how exoskeleton torque inputs can be incorporated into a tractable model.

Scope

This project is presented as a modeling and preliminary simulation study. Experimental validation of a complete model-based assistance controller is not claimed.
